Mindbody’s Built-In Waiver vs. a Dedicated Waiver App: What Studios Actually Need

Nov 3, 2026

Mindbody has a built-in waiver. See what it covers, where busy studios outgrow it, and when a dedicated app like WaiverKing saves front-desk time.

Do you need more than Mindbody's waiver?

If you run your business on Mindbody, you already have a waiver tool, and it just got better. For plenty of businesses, it's enough.

But if your front desk still has clipboards, a "to scan" pile, or duplicate client profiles to merge, it's worth asking whether the built-in option covers everything.

Here's what the built-in waiver does well, where busy studios outgrow it, and when a dedicated waiver app makes sense.

What Mindbody's built-in waiver does

Mindbody's own help docs describe two related tools on the Client Forms and Liability Waiver screen.

The waiver: you type your policy into a text box, and you can turn on a setting that asks clients to acknowledge it during booking. Clients agree to it before they create their account.

Client forms: custom forms you can include in appointment, class, or course confirmation emails, so clients fill them in ahead of time. A waiver can be attached to a client form.

When a client completes the waiver in the Mindbody app, the Mindbody business app, your branded app, or branded web tools, a digital copy is saved to their profile automatically. Staff can also mark a waiver as complete or email an unsigned waiver to a client from their profile.

What changed in October 2026: Mindbody's October 25, 2026 product update (www.mindbodyonline.com/business/product-updates) added expiration rules on waivers and forms with automatic reminders to re-sign, sending forms at more touchpoints such as profile creation or a membership purchase, required initials on key terms, and mapping form fields to the client profile, so details auto-fill on submit and forms pre-fill with info you already have, with changes logged.

That's a strong starting point, and it's worth turning these features on.

Where studios start to feel the limits

Mindbody's current help docs still point out a few things worth knowing.

Paperwork completed outside Mindbody still needs handling. Mindbody says waivers completed through other methods need to be uploaded manually or stored in hard copy. If walk-ins or guests sign on paper, someone still has to scan, upload, and file.

Client forms are optional before booking. Mindbody's help docs say clients aren't required to complete client forms before they enroll in services. The new reminders and extra send points should help, but some people will still arrive with forms to fill in.

Features depend on your package. Mindbody notes that some client form features are only available with certain software packages.

Real businesses have more than one form. A Pilates studio might need a waiver, a PAR-Q, and a membership agreement. The more forms you have, the more it matters how easy they are to build, complete, and find.

None of this makes the built-in option "bad." It just means the front desk may be doing work software could do.

So, which do you need?

Mindbody's built-in waiver may be enough if nearly all clients sign through Mindbody's own apps or web tools and you have one simple policy and few other forms.

A dedicated waiver app is worth a look if you still scan, upload, or file paper forms, walk-ins or groups slow down check-in, parents need to sign for children, you use several forms (PAR-Q, intake, membership, treatment notes), or your team spends time fixing duplicate client profiles.
